What "fuel savings cover the payment" means

MPkWh does not claim an electric car is free. This page compares fuel and electricity costs only. When avoided gasoline spending is equal to or greater than the EV's monthly payment plus its electricity, the fuel savings cover the payment — nothing more than that.

The calculation excludes insurance, registration, sales and use tax, maintenance, depreciation, financing opportunity cost, parking, and home charger installation. It also assumes you would actually stop driving the gas car, rather than adding the EV alongside it.

Break-even MPG is the gasoline fuel economy at which monthly gas spending exactly equals the EV's monthly payment plus electricity. It is monthly miles × price per gallon ÷ the EV's total monthly cost. A gas vehicle rated below that number burns enough fuel to cover the EV.

Two things move this number more than anything else: how far you drive and what you pay per gallon. At low mileage and cheap gasoline, break-even MPG falls into single digits, where essentially no production vehicle qualifies. At high mileage or high regional gas prices it rises into the range of full-size trucks and SUVs. That is why the percentage of the payment covered is the more useful headline: it always gives a real answer.

Every input on this page is editable. The defaults are national figures, and your own electricity rate, local gasoline price, and quoted payment will all differ.

How the numbers are calculated

Gas cost = (monthly miles ÷ combined MPG) × price per gallon, using the fuel grade the vehicle requires.

EV electricity = monthly miles ÷ Mi/kWh, increased by the charging overhead, multiplied by the electricity rate. MPkWh's Mi/kWh figure is EPA range divided by usable battery capacity, which measures battery-to-wheel efficiency — so the overhead is added here to reach the energy a household actually buys.

EV monthly cost = payment + electricity + any lease mileage overage. On the effective-lease basis, the payment also includes the amount due at signing and required fees amortized across the lease term. Taxes are excluded because they depend on location.

Mileage overage applies automatically when annualized mileage exceeds the lease allowance: excess miles × the per-mile fee ÷ 12. If you are consistently over the allowance, a purchase or finance arrangement is likely a better comparison than a lease.

Fuel and electricity costs only. Insurance, taxes, registration, maintenance, depreciation and other ownership costs are not included. Lease offers vary by credit tier, geography, inventory, trim and incentives — an advertised offer is not a quote.
