How Mi/kWh is calculated: EPA-rated range divided by usable battery capacity — the portion of the pack the car can actually access. This measures battery-to-wheel efficiency, not wall-to-wheel (which would add ~15% charging losses). Pack kWh is the total (gross) capacity; Usable kWh is what the car's software allows. Both columns are hidden on mobile.

Pack sizes sourced from ev-database.org and manufacturer spec pages. Tesla does not publicly disclose battery capacity — Tesla figures are estimated from third-party discharge tests and teardown data. The 2026 Nissan LEAF 75kWh pack size is estimated from historical DoD ratios; manufacturer specification not yet confirmed.

EPA range data sourced from fueleconomy.gov. Efficiency figures reflect the most efficient trim/configuration of each model. Table updates every Monday as new EPA data is published.

* Cost to drive 100 miles based on US avg. electricity rate of $0.1844/kWh (EIA, May 2026 — most recent published).
